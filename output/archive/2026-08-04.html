EU AI Act Kicks Into Action on August 2

The European Union has begun enforcing key aspects of its AI Act as of August 2. This includes new transparency requirements for AI systems, mandating labels for chatbots and AI-generated content, and prohibiting certain high-risk AI uses like non-consensual sexually explicit content generation. Non-compliance can lead to fines up to 15 million Euros or 3% of global annual turnover, and new complaint and whistleblower tools have been launched for enforcement.

Policy & business EU AI Act & California AI Transparency Act Converge

Both the EU AI Act and California AI Transparency Act came into force on August 2, 2026, establishing converging global transparency obligations for AI-generated content. These laws mandate that synthetic media, such as deepfakes, must be identifiable and traceable, with providers required to embed machine-readable markers and, in some cases, provide public detection tools. The EU Act imposes penalties up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, while California's law sets a $5,000 per violation fine.

Tools & products Skunk Works Advances Sensor-Powered AI Fighter Intercept

Lockheed Martin Skunk Works and the U.S. Air Force Test Pilot School demonstrated sensor-driven AI autonomy on an X-62 VISTA fighter jet. This project validated closing the sensor-to-action loop with AI in an operational combat aircraft and accelerated autonomy development, with full integration and ground testing occurring in just three months.
