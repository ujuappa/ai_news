Anthropic's Annualized Revenue Surges to $65B

Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate has surged to over $65 billion by the end of July, up from $47 billion in May and $9 billion at the end of 2025. This rapid growth surpasses that of rival OpenAI, which doubled its revenue to $40 billion in the same period. Investors expect Anthropic's revenue to continue accelerating, potentially reaching $100-$120 billion by the end of 2026, ahead of an anticipated IPO at a valuation of $2 trillion or more.

Policy & business Nvidia Invests $1.5B in OpenAI Data Center, $105B Credit

Nvidia is investing $1.5 billion in SB Energy, a data center developer linked to SoftBank and OpenAI, and will provide up to $105 billion in credit. This investment secures Nvidia as the sole compute infrastructure supplier for OpenAI's Ports-Pike data center, which could scale up to 8 gigawatts. The project includes building a $33 billion natural gas power plant, highlighting the immense infrastructure costs and energy demands of large-scale AI operations.

Groq has raised $350 million, led by Disruptive and with Nvidia's planned participation, valuing the company at $3.5 billion. Policy & business AirTag Reveals Amazon Trashing Rare Books to Train AI

An investigation using an AirTag revealed that Amazon is reportedly acquiring rare books, destroying their spines, and scanning them to train its AI models. This practice targets texts not readily available online, offering unique data valuable for improving large language models. The report raises ethical concerns about the destruction of physical cultural artifacts for commercial AI development.

Model releases Claude to Start Watermarking AI-Generated Text

Anthropic's Claude AI will begin watermarking AI-generated text to comply with EU regulations, which will involve changing how the chatbot makes small, random choices in its output. While intended for transparency and accountability, concerns are raised that this watermarking process could potentially degrade the quality or naturalness of Claude's generated text. The initiative reflects a broader industry trend toward implementing mechanisms to identify AI-created content.

Policy & business Amazon Destroying Rare Texts to Train AI

Amazon is reportedly destroying rare, physical books to scan their content for AI training data, as revealed by a tracking device placed in a book. This practice targets out-of-print or internet-scarce texts, providing unique, human-generated data crucial for preventing 'model collapse' in LLMs. The method raises ethical concerns about the preservation of cultural artifacts versus the demand for training data.
