Research AI Decodes Hidden 'On Switch' in Human DNA

Researchers at UC San Diego utilized AI to decode the DNA signature of a key genetic 'initiator' switch that regulates gene activation in humans. By analyzing 500,000 DNA sequences, the AI model identified this initiator in approximately 60% of human genes. This breakthrough could help predict the impact of harmful mutations and advance our understanding of gene regulation in diseases like cancer.

Policy & business Legality of AI Training on Copyrighted Books: It’s Complex

The legality of training AI models on copyrighted books is complex, with current copyright law, last updated in 1976, struggling to address modern AI challenges. A recent ruling against Anthropic, requiring a $1.5 billion settlement, clarified that while AI training itself was lawful, pirating books from illegal sources was not. Legal experts suggest this ruling might favor AI companies by distinguishing between "reading" and "copying" copyrighted material.

Research Inherent's Faraday Agent Outperforms Anthropic, OpenAI

Inherent, a London AI lab founded by DeepMind alumni, claims its new AI agent, Faraday, has outperformed larger models like Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5 at replicating scientific research. Faraday, running on a smaller 27-billion-parameter Qwen 3.6 model, achieved this through reinforcement learning, emphasizing "research taste" beyond mere accuracy. The company, which recently emerged from stealth with a $50 million seed round, aims to build AI that can discover new scientific knowledge.

Policy & business UCSB gets $20M federal grant for AI cloud lab

UC Santa Barbara has received a four-year, $20 million federal grant to establish an AI-programmable cloud laboratory at its OASIS facility. This lab will allow researchers nationwide to remotely access advanced AI equipment and conduct experiments. The initiative aims to democratize access to high-performance AI research infrastructure for U.S. organizations.

Policy & business Twitch and Amazon Sued for Training AI on Livestreams

Amazon and its streaming platform Twitch are facing a class-action lawsuit for allegedly using livestreams to train AI models without proper permission or compensation for creators. The lawsuit, brought by streamer Warren Pandiscia on behalf of millions, claims breach of contract, even though Twitch offered an opt-out feature that only applies to individual streams. Amazon, which acquired Twitch in 2014, has made AI a significant focus, but the lawsuit highlights concerns over data usage and creator rights.
