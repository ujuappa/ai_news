Policy & business US government sides with OpenAI on copyrighted material issue

The US government, under the Trump administration, sided with OpenAI in a lawsuit filed by The New York Times regarding the use of copyrighted material for LLM training. A 20-page brief was filed in defense of OpenAI, arguing that constraining LLM development based on a misunderstanding of fair use doctrine would hinder creative and scientific progress. This intervention supports the notion that training AI on copyrighted content without explicit permission could fall under fair use, aiming to foster a robust and competitive US AI industry.

Policy & business NYC to ban student AI use until high school

New York City is implementing a ban on AI use for public school students through eighth grade (typically ages 13-14), effective next week, as part of a major overhaul of technology policies for its 900,000 students. Additionally, classrooms will prohibit laptops or tablets for students through third grade (ages 9-10). This significant policy shift, closely watched nationwide, addresses concerns from teachers and parents about potential "cognitive surrender" due to AI use in early education.

Policy & business AI Infrastructure Investment to Hit US$31.6 Trillion by 2050

PwC projects that global investment in AI infrastructure will reach US$31.6 trillion by 2050, with annual data center capital expenditure rising from US$800 billion in 2026 to US$1.8 trillion by 2050. The US is expected to capture nearly half of this investment at US$15.1 trillion. Key factors influencing investment include energy access, connectivity, security, policy, and GPU availability, with ICT equipment's share of investment projected to increase from 70% to 93% by 2050.
