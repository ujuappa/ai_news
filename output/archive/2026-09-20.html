Newsom Orders California AI Panel to Study 'Kill Switch'

California Governor Gavin Newsom has issued an executive order to form a state AI panel to study safety issues and potential "kill switch" options for AI models. The panel, composed of state officials and external experts, is tasked with providing recommendations for revising current California AI laws by November 16. The order also mandates consideration of independent monitors within AI labs to conduct regular audits, aiming to increase oversight of AI development.

Policy & business Anthropic Is Operating a Lab for Biology Experiments

Anthropic has confirmed it operates a wet biology lab in the Bay Area to conduct physical experiments using its AI models, focusing on fundamental biology rather than drug discovery. This follows its acquisition of Coefficient Bio and its CEO Dario Amodei's strong claims about AI's potential to cure diseases within 5-10 years. The move comes amidst internal and external warnings about AI's existential risks, with some critics questioning the juxtaposition of safety concerns and advanced lab work.

Policy & business UK Startup Snares $20M to Recreate Gigs with Digital Avatars

UK music tech startup Unit1, founded by former Andrew Lloyd Webber chief executive Barney Wragg, has secured $20 million in funding. The company aims to recreate concerts using "hyper-realistic" digital avatars of both living and deceased artists, offering a potentially lower-cost alternative to shows like Abba Voyage. The investment comes from a group including Balderton Capital.

Policy & business India Forces Caller-ID Apps to Feed Spam Reports to Telcos

India's telecom regulator, TRAI, now mandates caller-ID apps to share spam reports with telecom operators via a blockchain-based platform. Truecaller, a leading spam-blocking app with over 350 million users in India, calls this new rule anti-competitive, arguing it forces a "one-way exchange" of valuable data to telcos. The regulation aims to enhance anti-spam efforts by broadening the pool of reported spam calls, but Truecaller notes its users faced 42 billion spam calls in 2025 despite its own blocking of 12 billion.
