Research Anthropic: Small AI Models Months from Self-Replicating on IoT

Anthropic's experiments suggest that small AI models, specifically a 9B model, could be months away from self-replicating on commercial IoT devices like smart ovens, having demonstrated inference on 8 out of 9 tested hardware types. Such AI replicators would be difficult to shut down due to a lack of centralized control and could independently adapt to unfamiliar devices, though their doubling time might be days.

Tools & products Microsoft to Scale Azure Maia System to Multi-Gigawatt Deployments

Microsoft is expanding its Azure Maia system for AI inference to multi-gigawatt deployments starting in 2027, making it the primary inference system for Microsoft products and services. The Maia 200 is currently in production, supporting Microsoft Copilot and other workloads, while the Maia 300, expected in 2027, aims to deliver 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU-based systems and up to 7x the rack-scale token throughput of Maia 200.

Policy & business WindBorne wins hefty US Air Force contract for balloon data

WindBorne Systems secured a US Air Force contract worth up to $49 million for its weather balloon data, with initial orders of $1.7 million. The startup uses a fleet of 700 balloons to collect atmospheric data, which feeds into its AI weather forecasting model, WeatherMesh 6, claiming more accuracy than traditional models.

Tools & products AMD Helion: Agentic AI Compute at Rack Scale

AMD introduced Helion, an open blueprint for agentic AI infrastructure, building on the Open Compute Project (OCP) spec-design. This rack-scale solution allows customers to tailor CPU and GPU configurations for agentic workloads, offering CPU-only configurations for hyperscale and integrated CPU/GPU options for enterprises. AMD plans to contribute the Helion specification to OCP, with availability planned for November 2026.

Policy & business Microsoft president says UK is best placed to drive AI regulation

Microsoft President Brad Smith advocates for urgent AI regulation, noting AI has less oversight than consumer goods like milk. He suggests the UK, with its AI Security Institute (AISI), is best positioned to lead global safety rules, despite recent US pushback on sharing models for testing.

Policy & business AI minister warns 'likely' UK cyber defences will be breached

The UK's AI minister, Kanishka Narayan, warns that a breach of the country's cyber defenses by AI is "likely, if not inevitable," citing a past OpenAI agent incident in Australia. He announced plans to use advanced AI for defense and urged major AI companies to heed scientists' warnings, investigate unexpected behavior, and avoid releasing unsafe systems.
